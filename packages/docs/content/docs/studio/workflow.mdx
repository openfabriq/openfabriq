The workflow page draws the applied factory as a graph. Read it after each `apply` to check that every step leads somewhere.

![The workflow graph: events lead to automations, whose outputs are the next events.](/screenshots/studio/workflow.png)

## Reading the graph

Each event points to the automation it starts. Each automation points to the events its rights produce, such as a label added, a pull request opened or a change request. Feedback loops appear as loops, for example *a maintainer answers* on a triage that asks questions. An automation with [`reviewed_by`](/factory/automations/#reviewed-by) has its reviewer beside it, in a loop: the change goes to the reviewer, and *needs changes* comes back, before anything leaves the machine.

The graph shows where work enters the factory (a person, GitHub, a schedule, Slack, Linear, Sentry or a webhook) and where it leaves: `needs-human`, `needs-info`, or a merged pull request. Nodes in a person's colors mark where someone has to act.

Click a node to open its panel beside the graph. For an automation: what starts it, what it may write on GitHub, and what can come next. For an event: who makes it happen and which automation picks it up. With nothing selected, the panel sums up where work starts, the automations, and what reaches you.

## Seen from an area

When the factory orders its agents by [area](/factory/overview/#a-large-factory-folders-by-area), a row of links above the graph shows it from one area:

- its automations stay, with the events they listen to and produce;
- the automations of other folders that produce what it listens to, or listen to what it produces, stay faded, with their area's name: the work comes from them or goes on there;
- the rest hides, including automations that only share its events.

The area is in the page's address (`?area=qa`), so you can share that view. *All* shows the whole graph again.

## What to look for

- A step that leads nowhere.
- A label that no automation listens to.
